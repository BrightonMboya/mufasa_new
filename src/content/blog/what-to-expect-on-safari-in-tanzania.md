## A Journey to Tanzania

Africa has a unique magic—sunsets so stunning they seem to stop time, remote places where the night sky fills you with awe, and moments with wildlife that change the way you see the world.

One of the best ways to experience Africa's natural beauty is through a wildlife safari. It lets you get up close to animals in their natural homes while also supporting conservation efforts in protected areas and national parks.

If you're choosing the perfect African safari destination, make Tanzania your top pick. A safari here is more than just a vacation—it's an unforgettable experience that will make you fall in love with nature and see the wild in a whole new light.

## What is a Safari?

### What is a safari and what makes it different from a typical vacation?

The word "safari" comes from Swahili, meaning "a journey," and is the root of the verb "to travel" in this East African language.

Tanzania is widely considered the best country in Africa for a safari. It has the largest national parks, UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and the highest mountain on the continent.

To further cement Tanzania's reputation as the top safari destination, Serengeti National Park has been named the "Best Safari Destination" year after year, beating out other national parks across Africa.

So, a safari in Tanzania is not just a vacation; it's a true travel experience. While places like Thailand or India may offer "safaris," those game drives don't compare to an authentic African safari, nor do they have the same historical connection to the Swahili word.

## Need Even More Reasons to Choose Tanzania as Your Safari Destination?

- The Great Wildebeest Migration has the largest movement of animals in the world (well, after the migration of bats, but it is not that spectacular)! And it occurs in Tanzania's Serengeti National Park for nine months a year!
- It is only in Tanzania where you can combine your safari adventure with climbing the highest mountain in Africa – Mount Kilimanjaro.
- Tanzania is the only location where one of the rarest gemstones in the world – Tanzanite – has ever been found.
- Tanzania has the largest population density of animals and diversity of animals compared to any other African country. Here, you are sure to witness healthy herds of elephants and can visit a rhino sanctuary where rangers are working to save the endangered black rhino species. Also, the largest population of lions on the planet is in Tanzania.
- While it is true that park fees to enter Tanzanian national parks are higher than neighboring countries, without question, the parks of Tanzania offer a higher-quality experience for an African safari.

## A Safari in Tanzania National Park Means:

- A typical African safari means a game drive under the guidance of a professional guide to designated protected areas. Your driver-guide will explain about the environment, animals and other wildlife you encounter and give the opportunity for you to take incredible photos of everything.
- In some locations, there is an opportunity for a 'walking safari' (such as in Arusha National Park), or a "Tree Top Walk" (Manyara National Park), which means you can hike or climb near the animals in a sustainable and environmentally-safe (and physically safe!) manner.
- Afterwards, you will continue to your accommodations at a comfortable lodge or camp, where you will enjoy a beautiful African sunset and eat a delicious meal as you reminisce about your exciting day.

## What is a Game Drive?

Safaris often involve long game drives, where guests can spend 6 to 8 hours in a safari vehicle to view animals, especially in parks where you can't leave the car except in designated areas.

Some guests prefer early morning game drives, leaving at sunrise to spot elusive animals like cheetahs, and then returning to the lodge earlier in the day to enjoy a swim or a relaxing hike before the evening.

There's no rule saying you have to stay in the park until closing time. National parks are open from 6 am to 6 pm, and you're free to come and go within those hours.

You don't have to spend all day in the car. Most lodges are located within or near national parks, so with a longer safari itinerary, you can enjoy the comforts of luxury accommodations throughout the day. Some lodges even have waterholes nearby, attracting animals right to your terrace, bringing the safari experience to you.

Since safaris often involve long hours in a vehicle, it's important to consider how well young children can handle the journey. While safaris are exciting and offer plenty to see—animals, birds, and unique wildlife—it can be tiring for younger travelers who may not be able to sit for long stretches.

Also, time moves differently on safari. A morning spent in Tarangire National Park, following a herd of elephants, will be thrilling. Your heart will race when you spot a lion just meters away, and you'll marvel at the height of a giraffe or try to get the perfect shot of a leopard in a tree. Before you know it, it's time for lunch. Your driver will take you to a scenic picnic spot, often overlooking a lake or with a stunning view of the park, where you can enjoy a meal outside of the safari car.

## Things That Make a Safari in Tanzania Special

### Animals

Tanzania has the largest biodiversity of animals in Africa, making it hands-down the best location for a wildlife safari. Considering even just Tanzania's northern parks and attractions, it is easy to see why Tanzania is a popular tourist destination for wildlife safaris:

**Ngorongoro Crater**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is known for its incredible population density of animals – the highest in the world per square meter. Visitors may also spy on the endangered black rhino in Ngorongoro Crater. Visiting this amazing conservation area offers guests the chance to see the most wildlife, and is truly a special experience in nature.

**Serengeti National Park** has also been named a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its unique ecosystem, and because it hosts the most ungulates on earth, and has the highest number of predators in a specific area than anywhere else in the world. Serengeti is where guests will see the wildebeest migration with their own eyes – with the opportunity to see it from the air in either a chartered plane or a hot air balloon adventure.

**At Manyara National Park**, guests have the chance to see tree-climbing lions, enormous numbers of pink flamingos, and hoards of other bird species, as well as one of the largest troops of baboons in Africa. Manyara National Park has recently constructed a series of connected bridges in the strong trees along Lake Manyara, allowing for a unique 'Tree Top Walk' to get even closer to the incredible birdlife of this park.

**Tarangire National Park** has one of the largest populations of elephants in the region, visitors are sure to see elephant families with young babies in this park. During the dry season, herds of zebras and wildebeests, and their predators – lions, cheetahs and leopards – make their way to Tarangire for its large river, as a source of water.

**Arusha National Park** is home to the iconic Mount Meru, the second-highest peak in Tanzania. Guests can experience a walking safari through the grasslands and get close to giraffes, zebras and herds of Cape Buffalo, all under the guidance of a professional park ranger, of course.

**Kilimanjaro National Park** is home to Africa's highest mountain, Mount Kilimanjaro. Climbing this incredible peak is a once-in-a-lifetime adventure for many.

## Scenery and Environment

Tanzania is one of the leading countries in Africa when it comes to setting aside large areas of land for conservation and wildlife protection. In fact, over 25% of the country is dedicated to national parks or protected conservation areas.

This means there are vast, untouched landscapes where nature thrives. You'll find remote areas where wild animals roam freely, like the incredible Great Migration, where millions of wildebeests risk crossing the Grumeti River, facing crocodiles and predators. You'll see savannahs where lions rule, and vast plains where elephants roam in their natural habitat.

In Tanzania, visitors can experience nature in its purest form, far from human development.

Photographers, both professional and amateur, often choose Tanzania for stunning nature photos. You can capture the endless African plains, snap a close-up of weaver birds building their nests in acacia trees, or get a shot of Africa's famous big cats, like cheetahs or serval cats, hunting in the tall grasses. Even without high-end gear, the vibrant scenery and active wildlife make every shot incredible.

It's no surprise that well-known wildlife documentaries, like those from National Geographic and Animal Planet, often film in Tanzania. The country's wildlife is so abundant and visible that they can capture amazing footage with almost guaranteed success.
